# Casting at home, hotels and conference venues

> Public copy. Evidence paths (`artifacts/...`) and linked internal reports are kept in the project's private archive; the measurements and decisions are summarised here.

Reviewed October 10, 2026. Required planning extension of CAST01 and C01 through C08. This document specifies intended behavior and fault fixtures; it does not establish implementation or physical venue acceptance.

The hotel/conference review reconfirmed Google's ordinary setup barriers and retired guest mode, SONIFI's room-scoped pairing/checkout disconnection, and the historical discovery-polling stutter report on October 10, 2026. These support the connection and cancellation requirements below; none proves GenX interoperability with an untested venue. The phone's network, receiver control path and receiver media path must each be observed independently.

## Product promise

GenX Streamer can make selection, connection attempts, deadlines and recovery deterministic. An unknown TV and third-party network cannot guarantee a successful cast. The TV needs a supported receiver, permission to use it, a reachable control path and access to compatible media. Discovery, sender internet access and receiver playback are separate observations. No devices found is a supported outcome, with an explanation and available alternatives.

## Connection flow

Keep one Cast dialog with a connection-method dropdown: Nearby TV, Venue pairing instructions, or Known receiver. Reveal only the chosen method's controls. Discovery remains explicit and bounded to 10 seconds. Do not add a permanent scanner or a large diagnostics screen.

1. Ask the user to complete the venue's normal Wi-Fi sign-in when needed. Explain that the TV may have its own pairing instructions. Do not intercept credentials, dismiss a portal as a stream failure, or change network/security settings.
2. For managed hotel casting, direct the user to the pairing instructions shown on their TV. QR pairing is supported only by the venue's system; GenX cannot invent a room PIN for generic Chromecast or DLNA. Opening a pairing page requires an explicit user action and visible destination; never treat arbitrary QR contents as trusted control endpoints. Then perform fresh supported discovery. Vendor integration must be separately proven.
3. List available receivers with friendly name, model, protocol and an identity discriminator. Duplicate names remain separate. Require explicit selection and confirmation against the TV's settings or venue pairing screen. Signal strength, alphabetical order, network address and room-like names do not establish which TV belongs to the user. When identity cannot be confirmed, show that uncertainty before sending media. A visual identification test is offered only if the receiver has a proven supported mechanism and the user explicitly requests it; never send tests to every TV.
4. Revalidate the selected identity and current network generation before connecting. Separate control reachability from media compatibility. A user-entered private receiver address can help when multicast discovery is unavailable, but cannot overcome blocked unicast traffic or prove TV identity. Keep that check non-playing.
5. Cast the user's selected channel only after selection/confirmation. LOAD acknowledgement remains Connecting. Matching owned receiver PLAYING status establishes reported playback; physical acceptance must also observe video/audio progress. Preserve the existing 20-second startup episode and 5-second stop deadline. Explain media/profile/access faults separately from discovery faults.
6. On network/interface change, venue pairing expiration, receiver identity change or takeover, invalidate pending operations and stop using stale credentials/addresses. Keep unresolved remote-stop ownership actionable. Never reconnect to a different receiver automatically. Provide explicit Stop and Forget this receiver; a travel session's receiver addresses/pairing material must not become global defaults.

## Media delivery away from home

The reported polling failure also has a traceable upstream fix: Home Assistant [PR 57991](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/pull/57991), merged October 18, 2021, upgraded PyChromecast to 9.3.0 and explicitly linked the TV stutter and JBL issues. Its change list restricted host-browser polling to audio hosts. This strengthens the evidence for controlling discovery traffic at its owner rather than merely hiding a receiver in the UI. It does not establish GenX hardware acceptance or justify copying an old dependency version. Retain a regression that closing discovery leaves no further discovery-owned inspection requests; active selected-session status remains separately owned.

Finding and confirming a receiver does not establish a usable media path. Before starting, choose one supported delivery path for the selected channel and receiver; do not silently alternate paths or repeatedly send LOAD commands.

Google's [Web Receiver documentation](https://developers.google.com/cast/docs/web_receiver/basic) explains a common phone-to-TV mismatch: native Android media access does not enforce browser CORS headers, while adaptive playback in a Cast Web Receiver does. Its [media requirements](https://developers.google.com/cast/docs/media) also vary by receiver. This is a documented compatibility boundary, not evidence that GenX has already passed receiver tests. Test a phone-playable HLS source whose playlist, segment, key or initialization resource denies the receiver origin; reject false success and retain the actual failed resource category without exposing its URL or credentials. Do not send private stream links through a public CORS proxy as a product workaround.

| Delivery path | Required capability and failure boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Receiver fetches the public stream directly | The receiver must independently reach the source and support its container, codecs and access requirements. Phone playback is insufficient proof. Do not forward the phone's cookies, authorization headers or credentials to an arbitrary receiver. |
| Sender serves a receiver-scoped relay | The running sender must expose a permitted, reachable local endpoint. Preserve validated upstream destinations, redirects, playlist/key/map/range handling and bounded cancellation. A Windows relay cannot provide this path when the traveler has only an Android phone; Android hosting needs its own implementation and lifecycle proof. Receiver HTTP consent remains separate from encrypted browser/phone sharing. |
| Venue-managed casting | Complete the venue's supported pairing procedure and verify that it exposes a compatible receiver/media path. A working YouTube or Netflix pairing is not proof that arbitrary HLS from GenX works; those applications may use different discovery or receiver applications. |

For Android travel acceptance, test screen locking and background transitions while a receiver plays. A direct receiver-fetch session and a phone-hosted relay have different lifecycle requirements. Preserve the foreground-only local-player policy; do not imply that it establishes a reliable background relay. If a supported hosting mechanism is absent or its required permission is denied, say so before starting that delivery path.

Expose a short outcome such as TV not found, TV confirmation needed, TV unreachable, or TV cannot play this channel. Explain only the observed failure: a timeout alone does not prove client isolation, an expired room pairing or an unsupported codec. Keep an optional details disclosure for the selected connection, with redacted diagnostics. Retain local playback and require an explicit user action for another attempt or delivery path.

The physical matrix must include receiver discovery with blocked upstream internet, phone-only provider access, receiver control with blocked sender hosting, network changes while relaying, and phone lock/background during an active session. These supplement the existing C01 through C08 tests; source planning does not establish a working Android relay or venue integration.

## Capability-specific fallbacks

When supported venue pairing exists, it is the preferred away-from-home route. Otherwise, direct Chromecast/DLNA requires a supported accessible receiver and permitted networking. A personal receiver on a permitted private travel network is another possible route, provided the TV permits its HDMI input, the network permits local peers and the receiver can reach the source. A phone hotspot is not universally sufficient: the sending phone may not behave as a peer on its own hotspot, and firmware/receiver requirements vary. Test the exact arrangement before treating it as supported.

A wired video connection is a fallback only when the phone supports video output and the TV permits that input. Wi-Fi Direct/Miracast is a separate capability and protocol; it must not be labelled Chromecast/DLNA or advertised on unsupported phones. Transcoding remains deferred. No alternative bypasses DRM, geographical restrictions or provider authorization. Local phone playback stays available when TV connection is unsupported.

## A simple travel decision path

The Cast dialog should ask for a connection method, not present a wall of troubleshooting buttons. Use this order when helping a traveler:

| What the traveler sees | Next action | What GenX can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Room TV displays venue casting instructions | Follow that venue's pairing flow, then explicitly refresh nearby receivers. | A supported receiver is exposed after pairing; venue authorization and room scoping remain vendor-owned. |
| Nearby receiver appears | Compare its displayed model and identity discriminator with the intended TV; explicitly confirm. | Fresh protocol identity matches the selected record. This is not cryptographic attestation or proof of room ownership. |
| Discovery is empty but venue supplies a supported receiver address | Use Known receiver, then perform non-playing validation and explicit confirmation. | Whether that supported endpoint is reachable; an address alone cannot identify a room or overcome client isolation. |
| No supported or reachable receiver | Explain the observed limit and offer capability-specific alternatives. | No false Playing state, random receiver selection, or endless retries. |

After selection, validate both control connectivity and the receiver's actual media state. A phone playing the channel does not prove that the TV can fetch or decode it. If the network changes, the receiver record changes, or the venue pairing expires, require fresh selection/confirmation. Keep travel selections session-scoped and provide Stop and Forget.

For conference venues, apply the same flow to the explicitly authorized presentation display. A conference receiver name or a familiar room number is not authorization; a managed presentation system may use protocols outside Chromecast/DLNA. The app must say when that connection type is unsupported.

### Travel readiness and location changes

Keep travel readiness inside the existing Cast dialog as a short progressive flow: Connect to venue Wi-Fi, Confirm TV, then Start channel. Show only the next relevant action. A completed step represents an observed fact, not a guarantee about the next step. Record the selected protocol, fresh receiver identity, network generation and supported media path in the owned session; do not infer readiness from a Wi-Fi name, signal strength, successful phone internet access or a previously working home receiver.

Treat arrival at another hotel, another conference room or a return home as a fresh connection when the network generation or receiver identity changes. Identical Wi-Fi names and reused private addresses must not preserve start authorization. Restoring preferences or reopening the app must not resume a travel cast automatically. Remembered friendly names may help the user recognize a receiver, but require fresh validation and explicit confirmation. Forget clears that remembered selection without pretending an unresolved remote session has stopped.

Before departure, offer a capability explanation for a personal receiver/private travel network or a wired connection only when the exact arrangement has been verified. A travel router is an optional user-supplied alternative, not an application dependency or a way to override venue restrictions. A venue can still block its upstream access or lock the TV's input. Android phone-only travel must be tested separately from Windows-hosted relay operation; a working PC relay does not establish portable phone casting.

This readiness flow and the additional fixtures below are planning requirements. They do not establish implemented step indicators, remembered-selection storage, or a tested travel-router arrangement.

The product success criterion is correct supported-device connection or a bounded, actionable unsupported outcome. A universal promise of finding the right TV and playing on it every time cannot be tested or delivered against equipment and networks controlled by others.

## Acceptance matrix

### Repeatable connection acceptance

Separate the result into receiver selection, control connection and channel playback. A discovered name is not confirmed room ownership, a successful control connection is not successful media delivery, and a working channel is not proof that every channel works on that receiver. Preserve these distinctions in redacted test receipts and in the optional connection details; the viewing flow needs only the next relevant action.

For each supported arrangement, record sender platform/build, receiver model/firmware, protocol, delivery path and network constraints. Re-run the same owned media fixture and selected-channel cases after adapter or discovery dependency changes. Home Assistant [issue 38188](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/issues/38188) reports discovery breaking after an upgrade even though an independent mDNS tool still saw device advertisements. The original reporter restored discovery by disabling Spotcast, then reported success with its updated version; another participant reported that removing Spotcast did not resolve their case. This is evidence for dependency interaction and regression testing, not a universal network remedy. GenX must keep one owned discovery implementation and test its actual receiver list and connection path after dependency updates. Independent network visibility must not be substituted for successful application discovery.

| Acceptance stage | Evidence required | Failure handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intended TV | Fresh selected receiver identity plus user confirmation against the physical TV or venue pairing instructions. | Duplicate names or unverifiable room ownership remain explicit uncertainty; no automatic media or identification test. Protocol identity is not cryptographic attestation. |
| Reachable receiver | Supported selected-endpoint control handshake within the existing deadline. | Show the observed connection failure, not a guessed cause such as isolation. Do not reuse an old address as identity or weaken authentication. |
| Compatible media | Matching owned receiver state and physical audiovisual progress for the tested channel/path. | Preserve local playback and distinguish source access, media compatibility and connection failures. Do not label phone playback as TV playback. |
| Safe departure | Confirmed Stop when reachable, expired local authorization and forgotten travel pairing material. | An unreachable TV leaves remote Stop unconfirmed. Forgetting local data cannot prove the TV stopped. Never issue commands against a replacement owner's session. |

Use a controlled compatible media fixture to distinguish receiver/path regressions from public-source outages in acceptance testing. In the product, any diagnostic media test must be explicitly requested for the already selected TV; never replace the user's channel silently. Google documents receiver-specific media and CORS requirements in [Supported Media](https://developers.google.com/cast/docs/media). A passing basic clip therefore does not establish HLS key/map/range, authentication, codec, live playback or all-channel support.

Report attempts and failures separately for ordinary Chromecast, DLNA, each proven venue integration and each proven personal travel arrangement. Include unsuccessful attempts rather than excluding them from a success rate. Do not collapse unsupported venue equipment into a connection PASS or advertise a percentage without the tested models, paths and network conditions. Physical venue proof remains required; these assertions are a test design, not completed acceptance.

### Deterministic travel rules

These are testable travel assertions under the existing C01 through C08 contracts, not a replacement or renumbering of the invariant register. Primary Google setup/discovery/guest-mode guidance, SONIFI room-pairing documentation and Home Assistant issue 55435 were rechecked October 10, 2026. The historical stutter report includes successful mitigation reports with a remaining initial dropped frame; it does not establish universal firmware compatibility.

| Assertion | Required result | Existing contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two receivers advertise the same friendly name. | Preserve both identities; never choose the first, nearest or room-like name automatically. Selection and confirmation precede media commands. | C01, C02 |
| A venue exposes no verifiable room identity. | Show uncertainty before an explicit user decision; never represent acknowledgment as proof of room ownership. A managed pairing system's room authorization remains separate from protocol identity. | C02, C08 |
| Discovery expires or multicast is blocked. | Finish the bounded discovery episode. A known-address check is explicit and non-playing; it neither enumerates address ranges nor bypasses blocked peer traffic. | C06, C08 |
| An address is reused or the network changes between confirmation and start. | Reject the old start authorization; require fresh receiver validation and confirmation. No automatic switch to another TV. | C02, C06 |
| Two venues use the same Wi-Fi name and private receiver address. | Bind confirmation to the current network generation and freshly observed receiver identity. Neither the Wi-Fi name nor the remembered address grants start authorization. | C02, C06 |
| A previous travel selection is restored on app launch. | No automatic media command. Revalidate and explicitly confirm the currently intended receiver. | C01, C02, C08 |
| A conference display advertises an unsupported presentation protocol or the TV input is locked. | Explain the unsupported connection; do not represent discovery, a pairing page or a wired accessory as established playback. | C03, C07 |
| The receiver accepts a media command but cannot fetch or decode the channel. | Stay Connecting until matching owned playback status, then report the observed failure within the startup deadline. Sender playback and command acknowledgment are insufficient. Physical acceptance additionally observes video/audio. | C03, C04, C07 |
| The playlist is reachable but a segment, encryption key or initialization resource fails receiver access/CORS. | No Playing result based only on playlist or LOAD success. Attribute only the observed resource failure; do not silently switch to a public proxy or leak source credentials. | C03, C06, C07 |
| The phone locks while the selected TV fetches directly, or while an explicitly supported phone relay is serving. | Test these paths independently. Direct fetch must not depend on a local decoder; relay support requires its own Android hosting/lifecycle proof. If hosting stops, expose interrupted delivery rather than claiming continued TV playback. | C01, C03, C04 |
| Discovery or a dialog closes while responses are still arriving. | Discard obsolete results and release owned work. Do not repeatedly poll unrelated receivers or send them identification media. | C01, C08 |
| A dependency update leaves advertisements visible but the application's receiver list empty. | Fail the supported discovery regression fixture; retain exact dependency versions and check the application's discovery owner before advising network changes. Do not introduce a second permanent discovery stack as a workaround. | C01, C07, C08 |
| Pairing expires, another sender takes over, or Stop cannot be confirmed. | Invalidate pending starts and expose the unresolved outcome. Never claim a confirmed remote stop or control another sender's session. | C02, C05 |

Venue instructions, protocol identity checks and receiver playback are distinct evidence. Integration tests must include both managed room pairing and ordinary supported receivers; a fixture pass or a successful home test does not establish hotel interoperability.

Use deterministic adapter/network fixtures for: zero receivers; duplicate names; spoofed/unverifiable identity; IP reassignment; multiple interfaces; Wi-Fi/VPN changes mid-connect; dropped mDNS/SSDP but reachable selected endpoint; unreachable endpoint despite discovery; captive-portal response; expired venue pairing; malformed or untrusted QR destination; control success/media fetch failure; unsupported codec; stalled receiver; foreground/background cancellation; takeover and unconfirmed Stop. Assert no wrong receiver command, no stale success, no unbounded retries and complete owned cleanup.

Also separate DLNA command, notification and media paths: allow control while blocking event callbacks; acknowledge a subscription but deliver no state; deliver late notifications from an obsolete session; permit SSDP while blocking commands; and permit commands while blocking the receiver's media fetch. Assert that stale or absent events cannot establish owned playback. If a supported targeted status query is used, prove its request budget and cancellation without polling unrelated venue devices. Review of the upstream documentation is planning evidence, not proof that these fixtures or physical notification paths already pass.

Physical acceptance is separate: Windows and Android with a real Chromecast and a real DLNA renderer, followed by a permitted managed venue pairing setup and a permitted isolated/private travel setup. Record receiver model/firmware, sender build/hash, observed path, deadlines, actual audiovisual progress and confirmed Stop. Do not store venue credentials, room numbers or pairing secrets in public receipts. Lack of receiver/venue access remains an open acceptance gate, not an app-development blocker.

## Existing implementation and planned work

Current source includes explicit bounded discovery, a non-playing known-address probe, owned receiver status and bounded Stop. Native receiver validation binds an expiring start handle to freshly observed protocol identity and network scope. The shared confirmation owner discards selection/confirmation handles and requests a fresh handle immediately before each start; obsolete operations cannot confirm a newer selection. Known or freshly unavailable identity requires explicit acknowledgment. Protocol-reported identity is not cryptographic attestation.

The Cast dialog source now offers Nearby TV, Venue instructions and Known receiver through one dropdown. Opening Cast searches for nearby TVs by itself, and selecting a verified TV starts casting at once (owner decision, October 19, 2026); only a TV whose identity cannot be rechecked still shows the review and its acknowledgment. Full screen offers the same Cast button in its player row on phones, handhelds and PCs. Closing, forgetting, changing methods or backgrounding invalidates pending confirmation. Active casting remains separately owned and requires Stop. Source and fixture evidence is recorded in implementation progress; fresh packaged and physical proofs remain necessary. Receiver device attestation, IPv6 discovery, secure receiver relay and physical compatibility remain open in [casting contracts](casting-invariants.md). T100 remains open for full implementation/acceptance. Venue instructions are guidance, not a tested vendor integration or a universal receiver promise.
